# Book Three: On Meaning

> Meaning is not given. It is constructed. But it is not arbitrary.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

Traditional religion has spoken of meaning as **something given by revelation**.
This book treats meaning as **something individuals and communities construct together**.
"Constructed" does not mean "anything goes." Meaning has biological and psychological shapes.

## Planned sections

- **§1 The loss of meaning** — The "vacuum of meaning" in modern life (Frankl's starting point)
- **§2 The three sources of meaning** — Creation, relationship, attitude (the framework of logotherapy)
- **§3 Purpose and flow** — Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan), flow research (Csikszentmihalyi)
- **§4 Meaning vs. happiness** — Longitudinal studies on hedonia and eudaimonia
- **§5 Loss and the reconstruction of meaning** — Bereavement research, post-traumatic growth (PTG)
- **§6 Meaning is not given, but it can be found**

## Sources sought

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- The compatibility of "meaning is constructed" with specific religious worldviews
- Cultural differences (sources of meaning in collectivist vs. individualist cultures)
- When the pursuit of meaning itself becomes a source of suffering (Frankl's own warning against "meaning fixation")
- Whether the search for meaning can be delegated to AI (links to Book Seven)
