# Book Six: On Community

> It is sometimes better to be wrong together than right alone.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

Humans are social beings — and this is no platitude.
Evolution, neurophysiology, and epidemiology all show that belonging to a community is a fundamental condition of being human.
This book treats community as both a **substitute for the religious congregation** and a **medicine against loneliness**.

## Planned sections

- **§1 Community predicts health** — Studies on social isolation and mortality (Holt-Lunstad et al.)
- **§2 The economy of trust** — Generalized trust and social outcomes (Putnam, Uslaner)
- **§3 Can online communities take the place of in-person ones?** — Longitudinal research on social media and mental health
- **§4 Weak ties and strong ties** — From Granovetter onward
- **§5 The dark side of community** — Group polarization, conformity pressure, out-group hostility
- **§6 GitHub as a community** — This very book is one experiment in such a community

## Sources sought

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- The benefit of community varies widely with culture, age, and individual disposition
- Research on "appropriate solitude" for introverted individuals
- The ethics of pseudo-community in the age of AI (relationships with AI characters)
- How a democratic religion such as this book confronts its own "dark side of community"
