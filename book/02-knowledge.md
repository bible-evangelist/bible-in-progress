# Book Two: On Knowing

> Verify before you believe. The method of verification is the highest commandment of this book.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

This chapter underwrites the methodology of the entire work.
What does it mean to be "based on evidence"?
On what grounds may we say we "know" something?
What hierarchies of knowledge exist, and where does irreducible uncertainty remain?

Traditional scripture located the authority of knowledge in **revelation**.
This book locates it in **falsifiability**.
The chapter examines that shift, and the difficulties that remain after it.

## Planned sections

- **§1 From revelation to verification** — The historical shift in the authority of knowledge (around the scientific revolution)
- **§2 Falsifiability** — Popper's criterion and its limits (Quine-Duhem thesis, etc.)
- **§3 The hierarchy of sources** — Meta-analyses, replication studies, single papers, expert opinion, anecdote
- **§4 The replication crisis** — Studies on replication failures in psychology and medicine
- **§5 AI's "knowing" vs. human knowing** — The asymmetry of generation and verification
- **§6 Knowing what we do not know** — Empirical research on intellectual humility

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- How to handle non-falsifiable claims (mathematics, logic, metaphysics)
- Where qualitative and phenomenological research fit in this book's evidence hierarchy
