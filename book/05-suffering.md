# Book Five: On Suffering

> Suffering is unavoidable. Its meaning, you may choose.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

Buddhism placed suffering at the head of the Four Noble Truths.
Christianity placed the cross at its center.
Confronting suffering is among religion's central functions.
This book, too, treats suffering as an object **to be faced**, not avoided.

## Planned sections

- **§1 The inevitability of suffering** — Statistical realities of chronic illness, loss, aging, and death
- **§2 The two-tier structure of suffering** — Primary pain and secondary pain (insights from mindfulness research)
- **§3 Clinical approaches to anxiety and depression** — Meta-analyses of CBT, effectiveness research on ACT
- **§4 How to think of death** — Death-acceptance research (Yalom and contemporary end-of-life studies)
- **§5 Empirical research on Buddhist meditation** — Clinical effects of mindfulness and compassion meditation
- **§6 Sharing suffering** — The neuroscience of compassion

## Sources sought

- RCT meta-analyses of mindfulness (e.g., Goyal 2014)
- Effectiveness research on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
- Studies on the relationship between death anxiety and meaning
- Neuroscience of empathy and compassion (Singer, Klimecki, etc.)
- Longitudinal research on the long-term effects of CBT

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- The actual effect sizes of mindfulness (and the possibility they are overstated)
- When "meaning-making about suffering" can intensify rather than ease suffering
- The ethical limits of suffering in euthanasia and palliative care
- Whether AI can "understand" suffering, or be a recipient of compassion
