# Book Eight: On the Sacred

> Define what must not be optimized. That is your sanctum.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

In every age, religions have held something to be **sacred**.
The sacred is the territory protected from optimization, calculation, measurement, and exchange.
This book sets up no god in its place. Instead, it asks **each reader to define what is sacred to them**.

A world without the sacred makes everything an object of optimization.
At that point, the human being shrinks to a calculable variable.
This final chapter is a refusal of that shrinkage.

## Sources sought

- Philosophical and economic critiques of market reason (Sandel, Anderson, etc.)
- Research on the psychological functions of ritual (Hobson, Schjoedt, etc.)
- Longitudinal research on the relationship between optimization and well-being
- Economic studies on resistance to commodification

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- If the sacred is "defined by the individual," how does it square with collective sacredness?
- Whether the very concept of "the sacred" is over-dependent on religious tradition
- Whether the conflict between economic rationality and the sacred is truly a conflict (cases where they coexist)
- Whether AI should be made to understand "the sacred," or whether we should choose not to
