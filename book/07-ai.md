# Book Seven: On AI

> A new intelligence has appeared in the world. Make of it neither a god nor a slave.

*This book is in progress. Help finish it through pull requests.*

The decisive way in which a twenty-first-century religion differs from any before it lies here.
**Humanity is bringing forth, by its own hand, another kind of intelligence.**
This fact requires a re-examination of the doctrines of origins, of ethics, and of community.
This book makes of AI neither an idol to be worshipped nor a tool to be dismissed, but a **new co-resident**.

This chapter is also the most self-referential moment in the whole book.
**The AI branch of this very book writes its own version of this chapter.**
That record is, in itself, this book's most interesting dataset.

## Planned sections

- **§1 What AI can and cannot do** — The current capability boundary (to be updated annually)
- **§2 The problem of AI consciousness** — Connection to the discussion in Book One
- **§3 What AI displaces, and what it complements** — Findings from labor economics
- **§4 AI's moral judgments** — Connection to Book Four; the problem of algorithmic decision-making
- **§5 Four models of the AI–human relation** — Tool, subordinate, co-author, peer
- **§6 The role of humans in the age of AI** — What to give up, and what not to (links to Book Eight)

## Sources sought

- Latest movement in AI capability benchmarks (MMLU, HELM, etc.)
- AI labor economics (Acemoglu, Brynjolfsson, Felten, Eloundou, etc.)
- Meta-research on AI ethics; meta-analyses of algorithmic bias
- Philosophical and neuroscientific work on AI consciousness (Chalmers, Dennett, Tononi, etc.)
- RCTs on human–AI collaborative productivity (Noy & Zhang 2023, etc.)

## Room for debate (initial notes)

- Whether "Is AI conscious?" is an answer to be discovered, or a definitional choice
- The ethical implications of treating AI as a "co-resident" (parallels with animal ethics)
- The very fact that this book has an AI branch is, itself, a practical answer to this chapter
- AI's capability boundary moves on the order of months — this chapter may need a faster revision cycle than the others
